# NMR / spin-ensemble quantum reservoir computing, and NMR techniques that could extend reservoir memory or expressiveness (2019-2026, emphasis 2023-2026)

Scope note: researched 2026-10-25 with about 20 search/fetch calls. Several primary full texts could not be read in full (PDF streams, figure-only tables). Those items are listed as gaps rather than guessed. "EXP" means experimental hardware, "SIM" means simulation or theory. "Synthetic" means NARMA, STM, parity and similar tasks. "Real-world" means datasets such as weather.

## (a) NMR / ensemble QRC papers: what each did and found

### Takeaway
Our simulated system is almost exactly a replica of **Hou et al., PRL 136, 120602 (2026)** (arXiv:2508.12383). That paper is the flagship liquid-state NMR QRC experiment: 13C-crotonic acid, 9 spins, Rx pulse-area encoding on the protons, 30 ms evolution for weather, proton FID with |FFT| giving 653 peak features, ridge readout, and a rewinding (input-replay) protocol. Apart from it, the NMR QRC literature is thin. There is one solid-state NMR experiment (Negoro et al. 2018, non-temporal tasks) and the Nakajima/Fujii ensemble theory (2017-2019). The other spin QRC papers are simulations of generic spin models; their memory results come from relaxation, dissipation and non-Markovianity, not from NMR-specific techniques. I found **no** published QRC work using spin echo/CPMG, singlet/long-lived states, GRAPE-learned reservoir pulses, or multiple-quantum readout.

### Cited Findings

**Hou, Hua, et al. (USTC/Fudan/CUHK), "High-Accuracy Temporal Prediction via Experimental Quantum Reservoir Computing in Correlated Spins", PRL (Mar 2026), arXiv:2508.12383. EXP, synthetic + real-world.**
- The system is 9 spins (4 13C + 5 1H) in 13C-labelled crotonic acid on a Bruker 400 MHz spectrometer at 298 K. Inputs are encoded in RF pulse area and the natural J-coupled dynamics act as the reservoir — [arXiv:2508.12383](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2508.12383); [PRL](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/r8ww-qw7j)
- Encoding is U = prod_{n=5..9} R_x^n[arcsin(s_k)] on the protons only, with s normalized to [0,1]. Pulses last about 10 us, so evolution during the pulse is neglected. For weather, temperature is encoded on the protons (theta) and humidity on the carbons (phi). Input dimension is limited to 2 or fewer. **Note: the HTML text reads arcsin(s), not arcsin(sqrt(s)). Check this against our g*arcsin(sqrt(s)) encoding before claiming replication** — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- The Hamiltonian is chemical shifts plus pairwise zz J-couplings, with Lindblad relaxation. τ = 0.3 s and 0.01 s for NARMA, τ = 0.03 s for weather. The authors state that T1 relaxation is important for performance — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- Readout: only the protons are measured, because the 4 carbons have low SNR. The carbons are treated as an "inaccessible bath", i.e. hidden memory nodes. A π/2 pulse precedes the FID, which is 8192 complex points at 0.3 ms. After the FFT, spectral peaks are selected to give **653 features**, with the H2O peak near 1880 Hz removed. The features are z-scored and fed to ridge regression with 10-fold CV. Appendix B argues that a single observable sampled over time spans a larger operator space, which raises the IPC upper bound — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- Back-action is handled by "rewinding": for each step k, the authors replay inputs k−n_wo..k. n_wo = 100 at τ = 0.3 s and 1000 at τ = 0.01 s, chosen from preliminary experiments and possibly larger than needed. This gives an experimental washout/memory scale: about 30 s of physical time at τ = 0.3 s and about 10 s at τ = 0.01 s — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- Results: best NMSE values are NARMA2 1.7e-7, NARMA5 3.4e-5, NARMA10 5.8e-5, NARMA15 6.4e-5 and NARMA20 4.3e-5, using the time-multiplexed FID readout. The single-time readout gives NARMA10 about 1.2e-3. Inputs are sums of sines, with 400 training and 100 test steps. Relaxation at τ = 0.3 s "diminishes high-order correlations", which reduces the time-multiplexing gain. A classical spin counterpart averages R² < 0.75 over NARMA2-20 — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- Weather task: Delhi daily climate 2013-2017 (Kaggle), with 374 washout, 600 training and 600 test steps, using a direct multi-horizon readout. For temperature, QRC roughly matches ESN(1000) at one step and beats the ESN average at longer horizons; with SVR post-processing it beats ESN(10000). For humidity it roughly matches ESN(10000). The ESN baseline saturates at about 5000 nodes — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- Appendix I (STM simulations): **unitary dynamics alone give zero STM, and dephasing (T2) alone is insufficient. Amplitude damping (T1) is what gives nonzero STM.** STM peaks at an intermediate τ. When T2 ≪ T1 the reservoir runs at very low polarization, so the signal is buried in noise. The authors suggest tuning T1/T2 relative to τ to match a task's memory depth, but they do not test it experimentally. Stated future work: pulse-engineered interactions, larger processors, and a weak-measurement protocol that avoids restarts — [arXiv HTML v2, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- The T2 values quoted in their Table 2 include inhomogeneity, so they are **T2***. The numeric table is an image and I could not extract it — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)

**Negoro, Mitarai, Fujii, Nakajima, Kitagawa, "Machine learning with controllable quantum dynamics of a nuclear spin ensemble in a solid", arXiv:1806.10910 (2018). EXP, synthetic, non-temporal (QELM).**
- The sample is l-alanine-1-13C diluted in a 2H7-l-alanine single crystal at 11.7 T and room temperature. The reservoir is 4 1H spins and the readout is 1 13C spin. Dipolar couplings exchange Z components. Hartmann-Hahn irradiation at 83 kHz decouples 2H and 14N — [ar5iv 1806.10910](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.10910)
- Input sets the RF phase as θ = arccos(2s−1). The readout is the **13C Z component (population readout)** after each of M = 11 evolution cycles, with L = 4 inputs giving 44 signals and pseudo-inverse regression — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.10910)
- MSE results: 2-bit XOR 2.0e-2, 3-bit XOR 2.8e-2, 4-bit XOR 0.2 (3 errors), multiplication 2.3e-3, and nonlinear I 3.1e-4. Performance improves with M. The run took about 4 h because 1H polarization is about 0.003%, requiring 16 accumulations with a 20 s wait. The tasks were not temporal; temporal tasks would need re-initialization before each input — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.10910); APS 2018 abstract: [APS](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2018/r15/10)

**Nakajima, Fujii, Negoro, Mitarai, Kitagawa, "Boosting computational power through spatial multiplexing in QRC", Phys. Rev. Applied 11, 034021 (2019), arXiv:1803.04574. SIM, aimed at NMR ensembles.** The idea is to combine several ensemble reservoirs (molecules) into one larger readout. Details were not retrieved — [PRApplied](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.11.034021); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.04574)

**Fujii & Nakajima, "Harnessing disordered ensemble quantum dynamics for machine learning", Phys. Rev. Applied 8, 024030 (2017). SIM.** This is the origin of the ensemble QRC idea and of time-multiplexed virtual nodes. Hou et al. cite it as the source of their time multiplexing — [arXiv 1602.08159](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1602.08159); [PRApplied](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.8.024030)

**Kutvonen, Fujii, Sagawa, "Optimizing a quantum reservoir computer for time series prediction", Sci. Rep. 10, 14687 (2020). SIM, synthetic + stock prices.** In a fully connected transverse-field Ising reservoir, varying the inter-spin couplings improves memory capacity, and there is an optimal timescale that maximizes it. Faster OTOC decay correlates with more accurate memory — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7477271/); [arXiv 1807.03947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.03947)

**Götting, Lohof, Gies, "Exploring quantum mechanical advantage for reservoir computing", Phys. Rev. A 108, 052427 (2023), arXiv:2302.03595. SIM, synthetic STM.** Entanglement and the occupied phase-space dimension are linked to linear STM capacity. High entanglement is needed to access a larger phase space and higher STM. The paper also quantifies how dephasing hurts physical reservoirs — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2302.03595)

**Martinez-Pena et al., "Information processing capacity of spin-based QRC systems", Cogn. Comput. (2023), arXiv:2010.06369. SIM.** This is an IPC analysis of Ising spin reservoirs — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06369)

**Mujal et al., "Time-series quantum reservoir computing with weak and projective measurements", npj QI (2023). SIM.** This is the source of the rewinding protocol and of the weak vs projective measurement trade-off. Hou et al. use its rewinding scheme — [npj QI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00682-z)

**Sannia, Ravell Rodriguez, Giorgi, Zambrini, "Non-Markovianity and memory enhancement in QRC", arXiv:2505.02491 (2025). SIM.** The authors prove that Markovian QRC architectures have exponentially decaying memory of past inputs, which bounds long-term memory. Non-Markovian reservoirs outperform them on tasks that mix short- and long-term correlations. They propose an embedding that tunes a reservoir from Markovian to non-Markovian — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02491)

**Sasaki et al., "Hamiltonian-driven architectures for non-Markovian QRC", arXiv:2505.14450 (2025). SIM.** The reservoir is split into system and environment under one joint Hamiltonian. This gives information backflow and slower STM decay than the Markovian limit, but strong non-Markovianity can violate the echo-state property, which makes linear readouts unstable — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14450)

**Čindrak et al., "Quantum reservoir computing maps data onto the Krylov space", arXiv:2502.12157 (2025). SIM.** Krylov observability is proposed as a fast proxy that tracks IPC. I found no Čindrak paper specifically on spin *ensembles* — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12157v1)

**Liouvillian analysis (arXiv:2609.23429, 2026). SIM.** Interference between coupling points can create decoherence-free subspaces that retain information for a long time, but they can break the echo-state property — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23429)

**NV / solid-state spin reservoirs.** "NV-QRC" (arXiv:2609.06292, 2026) proposes a dense NV ensemble as both magnetometer and reservoir for classifying non-repeatable field waveforms; the search snippets showed only the proposal, not hardware results — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06292). An HKUST page describes an experiment with a single NV electron as input/readout and the 13C nuclear bath as dark reservoir nodes; I could not confirm the year — [HKUST](https://physics.hkust.edu.hk/node/3815)

**Review: Tariq et al., "Quantum Reservoir Computing: Recent Advances and Future Directions", arXiv:2607.18552 (Jul 2026).** According to the search snippet, it notes a 5-spin molecular register read through the FID and a 9-spin time-multiplexed experiment (Hou et al.) that must replay the input sequence for every prediction, which adds cost — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18552)

### Inferences
- Our simulation closely replicates Hou et al. (9 spins, protons read, 30 ms, 653 FID peaks). Their published long-horizon weather advantage over ESN is a direct benchmark for us. Our "input overwrite" weakness matches their own observation that relaxation at long τ kills high-order correlations, and that Rx on all protons at every step rewrites the readable spins.
- In Hou et al., **the carbons are already a partial "store" register**: for single-input tasks they are not pulsed and not read, and they carry memory only through J-coupling back to the protons. "Memory spins left unpulsed" is therefore an existing design, which can be pushed further, not a new one. The natural next step is to put the store on carbons whose information returns to the protons through J (~35-72 Hz C-C; 1J_CH ~ 125-160 Hz is typical for C-H, but this figure is not from a fetched source).
- Hou's Appendix I says T1, not T2, is the memory resource. That suggests our long-horizon memory is limited by how fast z-populations are re-mixed and overwritten by each Rx pulse, more than by T2*.
- Sannia et al.'s theorem means any Markovian Lindblad reservoir, ours included, has exponentially fading memory. Every technique in section (b) can only lengthen the decay constant, not change its form, unless it adds a non-Markovian component (for example an unread spin "environment" with long T1).

### Gaps
- Exact Table 2 parameters of Hou et al. (shifts, J, T1, T2*) could not be extracted, since the table is an image. Our 0.20-0.33 s T2* and 2.2-27.5 s T1 are presumably from it, but I did not verify this.
- No published paper on "Domingo, Carlo, Mujal" as an NMR/ensemble QRC trio was found in this search budget.
- No 2024-2026 liquid-state NMR QRC experiment other than Hou et al. was found. No NV-ensemble temporal-QRC hardware result was found.
- No paper directly studies ensemble-averaged (no shot noise, no back-action) readout as a QRC advantage, beyond Hou's weak-measurement framing and Mujal et al.

## (b) Feasibility and expected memory gain of each untested idea (with NMR literature values)

### Takeaway
The largest physically grounded lever is **storing information in z-populations / longitudinal order (T1 = 2-27 s) instead of transverse coherence (T2* ≈ 0.25 s)**. At τ = 30 ms that is a ~70-900-step decay constant against ~7-11 steps. True T2 from refocusing gives only about 3x (crotonic acid: literature T2 ≈ 0.7-1.1 s against T2* ≈ 0.2-0.33 s). That gain is **not reachable in our Markovian simulation simply by changing a rate**, because inhomogeneous T2* is static and reversible. Singlet/long-lived states, 12-spin molecules, 19F systems and GRAPE-learned pulses are physically real but have no QRC precedent. All of these gains are inferences, not published QRC results.

### Cited Findings

**T2 vs T2* and refocusing (spin echo / CPMG)**
- 1/T2* = 1/T2 + 1/T2inh, and the linewidth is 1/(π T2*). A CPMG echo train refocuses static inhomogeneity, so the echo decay reflects the true T2 — [Northwestern IMSERC](https://imserc.northwestern.edu/guide/eNMR/eNMR1D/cpmg.html); [Weizmann](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/ChemicalResearchSupport/units/nmr-oc/methods/relaxation-measurements)
- Crotonic acid true T2 (7-qubit sample, 1210.4760): C1-C4 measured 1.09, 0.92, 0.84 and 0.97 s; methyl and protons about 0.7-0.8 s. Another study (2609.05248) reports carbons at about 0.66-0.92 s. Values vary with sample and spectrometer — [arXiv 1210.4760](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.4760); [arXiv 2609.05248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05248)
- Hou et al. explicitly say their Table 2 T2 includes inhomogeneity, i.e. T2* — [arXiv 2508.12383](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)
- Hou Appendix I: dephasing alone gives no STM, and STM comes from amplitude damping (T1) — [arXiv 2508.12383](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12383v2)

**Crotonic acid couplings (the current system)**
- C3-C4 J is about 72.2-72.3 Hz and C2-C3 J about 69.6-69.7 Hz. One 2026 table gives C3-C4 as 36.12 Hz, so the sources disagree on assignment or value. The methyl group is three equivalent protons with spin-3/2 and 1/2 subspaces, described as a "six and a half" qubit system — [arXiv 1210.4760](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.4760); [arXiv 0803.1982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1982); [arXiv 2609.05248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05248)

**Larger molecules**
- Negrevergne et al., "Benchmarking quantum control methods on a 12-qubit system" (PRL 2006, quant-ph/0603248): liquid-state NMR, a 12-coherence state, and universal control on up to 11 qubits plus 1 qutrit. The snippets did not show the molecule (commonly reported as 13C/15N-labelled l-histidine) or its couplings — [arXiv quant-ph/0603248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0603248)
- Perfluorobutadienyl iron complex (Vandersypen et al., Shor's algorithm, 2001): 5 19F + 2 13C spins, about 0.88 mol% in diethyl-ether-d10 at 30 °C, synthesis yield about 5%. J-values appear only in the figures — [arXiv quant-ph/0112176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0112176); [Jones review 2402.01308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01308)
- 19F-19F couplings in bromotrifluoroethylene (a different molecule) are about −122, 75 and 54 Hz, which shows 19F J can be about 2x larger than C-C J — [arXiv quant-ph/9905041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9905041)
- The Jones review "Controlling NMR spin systems for quantum computation" (Prog. NMR Spectrosc. 140-141, 49-85, 2024) is the current canonical reference for molecules, GRAPE and relaxation (633 refs). It notes that 13C-chloroform has short T2 and long T1, and that labelled sodium formate is better balanced — [arXiv 2402.01308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01308)

**Long-lived (singlet) states**
- Singlet lifetimes T_S can exceed T1 by an order of magnitude or more, up to 37 T1 in thermally polarized samples. Designed 13C2 molecules reach T_S > 10 min in solution, and 15N2O reaches about 25 min. The 13C singlet decay mechanisms are fluctuating CSA and leakage from slightly broken magnetic equivalence — [Levitt Annu. Rev. Phys. Chem. 63, 89 (2012)](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/347311); [arXiv 1201.1482](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1201.1482); [Soton 13C singlet](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/345404/1/ja3089873-1.pdf); [Southampton project page](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/chemistry/research/projects/long_lived_nuclear_singlet_states.page)

**Multiple-quantum / heteronuclear coherence**
- 13C-1H two-spin (multiple-quantum) coherence can decay more slowly than single-spin coherence (Grzesiek & Bax 1995). Methyl-group multiple-quantum coherences couple differently to a remote spin — [Bax lab PDF](https://spin.niddk.nih.gov/bax/lit/508/232.pdf); [arXiv quant-ph/0410140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0410140v2)

**Optimal control / learned pulses**
- GRAPE is the standard NMR optimal-control method, using piecewise-constant controls on top of a drift Hamiltonian. A 2024-25 paper trains a neural network on GRAPE pulses for a 3-qubit benchtop NMR. **No QRC work using GRAPE or learned reservoir pulses was found** — [arXiv 2412.05856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05856v3); [arXiv 1308.4615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.4615)

**Other spin-reservoir memory levers (SIM)**
- Tunable local dissipation improves linear and nonlinear memory over discontinuous erasing maps. Tuning couplings yields up to about 50% higher memory capacity. Non-Markovian embedding breaks the exponential-decay bound — [arXiv 2212.12078](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12078); [arXiv 2505.02491](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02491)

### Inferences (feasibility and expected gain per idea; my estimates, not published)
At τ = 30 ms per step, a quantity with lifetime T survives about T/τ steps (1/e).

1. **Spin echo / CPMG (T2* to T2).** Transverse memory changes from 0.20-0.33 s (about 7-11 steps) to 0.7-1.1 s (about 23-37 steps), roughly 3x, and only in physical hardware. *Critical caveat for our simulation:* if our Lindblad model folds the inhomogeneous T2* into Markovian dephasing, a simulated echo cannot recover anything. "Refocusing" would then amount to editing the dephasing rate, which is a modelling choice and not a technique. A faithful test needs an explicit static inhomogeneity distribution, for example averaging over a field-offset ensemble. Side effects: a 180° pulse also refocuses chemical shifts and heteronuclear J, unless applied selectively, and that removes part of the dynamics that generates features. Homonuclear J is not refocused. Since Hou shows dephasing contributes no STM, the gain in useful memory may be well below 3x. Feasibility: trivial on a spectrometer, with high pulse-count error accumulation for long CPMG trains.
2. **Population (z) storage / readout.** T1 = 2.2-27.5 s gives about 70-900 steps. This is the largest available reservoir of memory and matches Hou's finding that T1 drives STM. Options: (i) small flip angle g ≪ 1 so each Rx perturbs z only slightly (less overwrite; cost: lower nonlinearity and SNR); (ii) read z via a π/2 read pulse on a copy, which is free in simulation, while physically it needs rewinding or a separate scan (Negoro used 13C Z readout); (iii) 13C-channel readout, which physically has ~1/4 the gamma and ~1.6% natural sensitivity but is 13C-labelled here, so it is SNR-limited as Hou noted, and free in simulation. Risk: very long T1 memory against fading memory and the echo-state property. Hou's n_wo = 100-1000 washout already reflects this.
3. **Write/store separation (unpulsed memory spins).** Already implicit (the carbons are an unread bath in Hou). Making the carbons the store is a plausible test: pulse only the protons, let the carbons keep z-order with T1 of several seconds, and transfer it through J_CH/J_CC. Expected effect: a slower-decaying, non-Markovian-like memory as seen from the proton subsystem, along the lines of the Sasaki/Sannia system-environment split. No published magnitude exists.
4. **zz-order / longitudinal two-spin order.** Two-spin order 2IzSz relaxes on a T1-like timescale (seconds, not T2*), and unlike single-spin z it is invariant under global Rx(π) up to sign. It is a natural nonlinear (product) memory carrier. No QRC literature exists, and I found no crotonic-acid-specific relaxation value.
5. **Singlet / long-lived states.** T_S ≫ T1 (10x to 37x) needs a near-magnetically-equivalent pair or spin locking. The crotonic acid carbons are strongly inequivalent at 400 MHz, so the singlet is not an eigenstate without a spin lock or a special molecule. This conflicts with "free J evolution between pulses". It may be useful as a very long (minutes) slow-memory channel, but it would likely violate fading memory unless deliberately leaked. Low priority.
6. **10-12-spin molecules (for example 13C/15N histidine, the 12-qubit benchmark).** The Hilbert space and FID peak count grow, which may improve expressiveness. Memory depends on per-spin T1/T2 more than on size. Simulation cost: exact Lindblad on 12 spins means a 4096x4096 density matrix, about 64x the 9-spin cost, so GPU budget must be considered. The molecule's parameters must be sourced (gap).
7. **19F systems (stronger coupling).** 19F J can be about 50-120 Hz homonuclear, against about 35-72 Hz C-C, and 19F has high gamma (good SNR, observable FID, unlike 13C). The 7-spin perfluorobutadienyl Fe complex is a proven QC molecule but is hard to synthesize (5% yield). Faster J mixing per 30 ms step increases mixing and nonlinearity, but does not by itself lengthen memory, which is T1-limited.
8. **GRAPE / learned control between inputs.** No QRC precedent. Hou lists "pulse-engineered interactions" as future work. In simulation it is cheap to try periodic decoupling or recoupling blocks (for example selectively decoupling the store spins during write pulses). Fully learned pulses risk losing the "natural dynamics" selling point and overfitting.
9. **Multiple-quantum or heteronuclear readout.** MQ coherences are not directly observable and need a reconversion pulse block. In simulation, ⟨multi-spin operators⟩ are free extra features. Physically, heteronuclear MQ can decay more slowly than SQ (Bax). The expected gain is expressiveness (higher-order correlation features), not memory.

### Gaps
- No literature value of crotonic acid T2 measured under the *same* conditions as Hou's T2* table, so the 3x ratio mixes different samples.
- No published relaxation rates for zz-order in crotonic acid.
- 12-qubit histidine and 7-19F complex J/T2 tables are in figures I could not extract. They should be pulled from Negrevergne 2006 and Vandersypen 2001 / Vandersypen thesis (quant-ph/0205193) directly.
- No experimental or simulated QRC study of echo/CPMG, singlets, MQ readout, or GRAPE-designed reservoir pulses exists in my search. All memory-gain numbers above are lifetime-ratio estimates, not measured QRC memory capacities.
- I found no published method for handling input drift in NMR QRC. Drift was not addressed in any paper found.
