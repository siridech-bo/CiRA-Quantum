verify a Mølmer–Sørensen gate + its calibration before hardware — 2–4 ions, real modes
Validation ↔ Data 🌀 Smooth×GBC ← Visualizer Library

Pick a chain size, a driven ion pair, and a laser calibration. The verifier builds the real normal-mode spectrum (Coulomb chain), drives the COM mode to loop closure (Θ=π/8), and reports the honest Bell fidelity — splitting the error into which mode's motional loop failed to close and any coherent phase shift. Analytic, pulse-level, cross-validated against the Lindblad integrator to 1e-9. No hardware required.

1 · Loop closure — phase-space trajectories

Each motional mode traces a loop in phase space under the spin-dependent force (shown for its worst-case spin sector). The gate is clean only if every loop returns to the origin at the gate time. A loop that ends away from origin = residual spin–motion entanglement = gate error. COM (driven) closes by construction; spectators generally don't.

2 · Calibration landscape — F(δ_COM, Ω)

Bell fidelity over drive detuning (x) and drive strength (y), each column evaluated at its own closure time τ=2πK/δ. The bright ridge is where COM closes with the right phase; spectator modes carve dark notches into it. The ✕ marks your current operating point.

3 · Error attribution

Where the infidelity comes from: the coherent phase error (Θ≠π/8, incl. spectator phase) and each mode's residual-motion decoherence. The tall bar is the mode to fix (cool it, or move the drive / shape the pulse).

4 · Designed shaped pulse — close every mode

Turn verify into design. A piecewise-constant Ω(t) (2M+1 segments) is solved so all motional loops close simultaneously and the entangling phase lands on ±π/8 — recovering fidelity a constant drive can't.
