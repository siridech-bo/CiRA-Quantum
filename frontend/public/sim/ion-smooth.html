Smooth gate × GBC — combining playground

adiabatic (AESE) motional robustness + generator-based center-line compensation — where does combining pay off?
Validation Verifier

Two robustness tools act on different error axes. The smooth gate (Hughes et al. 2025) ramps the detuning δ(t) adiabatically so residual spin–motion entanglement is eliminated — robustness to the symmetric (mode-frequency / temperature) axis. GBC cancels the asymmetric (center-line σ_z) error to ε⁴. This page combines them and shows which of the four schemes wins (plain / smooth / GBC / smooth+GBC) as you dial the two error axes — and the threshold at which combining becomes worth it. All quantities are dimensionless (δ_min ≡ 1); a leading-order model built on the validated ion-smooth/ion-gbc engines.

1 · Combining win-map — which scheme wins over (Δδ, Δω)

The headline. For every symmetric error Δδ (x) and asymmetric error Δω (y), the color shows the lowest-infidelity scheme. The ✕ is your operating point. Plain wins when both errors are small; smooth when the symmetric/temperature axis dominates; GBC when the center-line axis dominates; smooth+GBC only in the corner where both are large enough to beat the combined gate-time penalty — the combining region.

2 · Net infidelity vs Δω (center-line error) — the thresholds

A vertical slice of the map at your current Δδ, n̄, and noise: the four schemes vs the center-line error Δω. Where two curves cross is a threshold — e.g. the Δω at which adding GBC to the smooth gate starts to pay off. Because the smooth gate is long, its center-line error grows fast, so GBC-on-smooth crosses in at a smaller Δω than GBC-on-plain.

3 · δ(t) & Ω(t) schedule

The smooth-gate ramp: Ω on at δ_max, sweep δ→δ_min (fast near δ_max, slow near δ_min), hold, sweep back, Ω off.

4 · Phase-space α(t) — DESE vs AESE

The forced-state displacement. DESE (diabatic) traces a loop; the smooth gate (AESE) collapses to a narrow line and returns to the origin — and stays closed under a Δδ offset (dashed), the robustness.

5 · Filter function F(ω) — motional-noise sensitivity

Sensitivity to a mode-frequency fluctuation at frequency ω (lower = more robust). The smooth gate (AESE) is orders of magnitude below the constant-δ (DESE) gate at low ω — the mechanism behind its temperature/motional robustness (Hughes et al. Fig. 2).
