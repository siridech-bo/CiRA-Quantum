Pound–Drever–Hall lock simulator

how a laser is stabilized to a Fabry–Pérot cavity — real physics
← Visualizer PDH refs ↗

PDH locks a laser's frequency to a reference cavity. The chain: an EOM puts RF sidebands on the light, the Fabry–Pérot cavity reflects it, a photodiode + mixer turn the reflection into a dispersive error signal, and a servo feeds back to the laser (fast diode current + slow PZT) to hold it on resonance. Modeled after Wang, Subhankar & Britton, Appl. Phys. B 131, 146 (2025).

0 · Fabry–Pérot cavity — the reference

The cavity the laser locks to. Transmission is the Airy comb: sharp resonances spaced by the FSR, width set by the finesse. The reflected-field phase swings through resonance — that dispersive phase is what PDH reads.

Raise ℱ and the resonance sharpens (narrower linewidth = FSR/ℱ) — a steeper discriminant, but a smaller capture range.

1 · EOM sidebands → PDH error signal

The EOM phase-modulates the laser at Ω, adding sidebands (Bessel J₀, J₁). The cavity reflection is demodulated (mix at Ω, low-pass) into the error signal ε(ν) — antisymmetric through resonance, so it tells the servo which way the laser has drifted.

Set φ wrong (→90°) and the steep discriminant flattens/mixes with the absorptive signal — the lock loses its sign. Keep Ω ≫ linewidth for the clean 3-zero-crossing shape.

2 · Closed-loop lock — fast current + slow PZT

The error drives a PI/PID servo split into a fast path (diode current, wide bandwidth) and a slow path (PZT, offloads DC). Watch the laser frequency vs time: free-running it wanders; engage the lock and it's pulled onto resonance and held. Residual = lock quality.

Crank P gain up (with the loop delay) and the servo oscillates — the stability limit. Too little gain → poor noise suppression. A big Kick past the capture range → unlock.

3 · VNA / loop dynamics — Bode & phase margin

The same loop in the frequency domain (what a VNA measures). Open-loop response L(f) = controller × actuator × cavity × delay. Where the gain crosses 0 dB is the unity-gain bandwidth; the phase there sets the phase margin. Push gain or delay and the margin vanishes — the oscillation of Panel 2.

Raise gain to push the bandwidth up — but the phase margin shrinks. Add delay and the phase rolls off fast: PM → 0 is the servo oscillation in Panel 2.

4 · AOM / AOD — the actuator

How an RF signal becomes a frequency correction. An AOM diffracts the beam off an RF sound wave: the +1 order is frequency-shifted by the drive f and deflected by an angle ∝ f. In double-pass (cat-eye) the shift doubles and the output stays collinear — the fast, drift-free frequency actuator. As an AOD, the same angle-vs-f steers the beam.
Acousto-optic
RF drive f (MHz)
Change f and the +1 order's deflection and frequency shift both move. Single-pass shifts by +f; double-pass by +2f with an angle-independent output — why PDH uses a double-passed AOM for fast frequency control.
